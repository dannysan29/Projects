- There's a planet called HD 189733b where it rains molten glass sideways due to winds blowing over 5,400 mph. Its deep blue color might look Earth-like, but it's actually due to silicate particles in the atmosphere scattering blue light -- basically, a glass-shard hurricane.

https://www.space.com/22614-blue-alien-planet-glass-rain.html

- Valles Marineris on Mars is so massive that it would stretch from New York to Los Angeles -- it's over 4,000 km long and up to 7 km deep, making it the largest canyon in the solar system. Some scientists believe it may have formed not just from tectonic activity, but also from the collapse of underground lava tubes, like a planetary-scale cave-in.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377027314000316

- If you could somehow sniff a neutron star (ignoring the whole death-by-gravity thing), it would smell like burnt gunpowder and nuclear decay -- a scent from compressed atomic particles. But you'd never get close: a teaspoon of its material weighs as much a Mount Everest.

https://www.nustar.caltech.edu/page/neutron-stars

- Venus Rotates in the Opposite direction of most planets and takes 243 Earth days to complete one spin -- but its year is only 225 Earth days long. That means a day on Venus is actually longer than its year.

https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/

- NASA detected sound waves from a black hole in the Perseus galaxy cluster, and translated them into audible tones. The result is an eerie cosmic hum in B-flat, about 57 octaves below middle C, far beyond human hearing.

https://www.nasa.gov/universe/new-nasa-black-hole-sonifications-with-a-remix/

- Jupiter's interior has helium rain that falls through liquid metallic hydrogen -- basically alien lava lamp physics. This strange rain is thought to play a role in powering Jupiter's massive magnetic field.

https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-facts/

- The Sagittarius B2 cloud near the Milky Way's center smells like rum and raspberries -- it's packed with ethyl formate, the same chemical that gives raspberries their flavor. Space smells weird, and now apparently delicious.

https://www.theguardian.com/science/2009/apr/21/space-raspberries-amino-acids-astrobiology
